# Education and training

## Methodology

Identify learner background, usage (live, self-study or handout), current difficulty and observable learning outcome. Structure as orientation → explanation → worked example → practice/feedback → transfer where relevant; short tasks may combine or omit stages.

Define unfamiliar terms before relying on them. Demonstrations show intermediate decisions, not only answers. Exercises specify input, action and success criteria; fictional practice material must be labeled. Distinguish common mistakes from valid alternatives.

Self-study pages need enough explanation to stand alone. Live pages may use staged disclosure when useful. Speaker notes remain opt-in.

## Visual defaults

Choose the learning action before the layout: concept/definition, worked example, comparison, procedure, exercise or reference. Keep terminology, semantic colors and navigational cues consistent.

Evidence diagrams and tables come from external tools/user assets. Use native annotations to direct attention without obscuring the figure. Whitespace may separate steps or allow practice; do not invent extra content or shrink type to fill it.

## Acceptance

Can the learner explain or perform the intended task, see a worked example, recognize the main mistake and determine whether their answer/action is correct? Are independent-reading pages understandable without an absent presenter?
