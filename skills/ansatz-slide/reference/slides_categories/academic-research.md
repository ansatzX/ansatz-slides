# Academic research

For group meetings/progress reports, also read [the group-report guide](../group-report-guide.md). Its methodology is separate from its default visual language.

## Methodology

Explain the question and motivation, relevant evidence gap, method, observations, interpretation, limitations and next work. A progress report may have no successful result; describe what was attempted, what was observed and what remains unresolved.

Keep equations with necessary conditions and symbol definitions. Figures retain axes, units, sample/statistical basis and sources where applicable. Distinguish measured results from analytic examples, hypotheses and generated illustrations. Do not invent experiments, confidence intervals or citations.

Match detail to the task: theoretical work emphasizes definitions and reasoning; experimental work emphasizes observations and controls; engineering research emphasizes mechanisms and measured behavior; humanities work emphasizes contextualized primary evidence.

## Visual defaults

Use readable figures with concise interpretation and consistent source/page markers. Body pages may be white, light or another user-specified background; no discipline has a prohibited default color. Do not recolor scientific evidence merely to match a theme.

Use available Latin/East Asian fonts; equation typography must use a supported representation, not an assumed LaTeX loader. Keep explanatory text editable where possible. Disclose any externally rendered equation's editability limit.

## Acceptance

Are question, method, evidence, interpretation and uncertainty distinct? Can the audience understand the main report logic early, inspect the figures and identify the next experiment or help needed? Never fill a sparse results page with invented success.
