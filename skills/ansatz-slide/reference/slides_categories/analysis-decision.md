# Analysis and decision

## Methodology

Organize around the decision: question → evaluation criteria → evidence/options → trade-offs → recommendation and conditions. Use a claim-led heading when the evidence supports a claim; a framing question is appropriate when the answer is not yet known.

Preserve dates, units, measurement basis, uncertainty and source. Separate observations, forecasts, assumptions and recommendations. Compare options on the same basis. Do not add unrequested research or invent quantitative depth.

## Visual defaults

For reading decks, supporting detail may be denser than live slides. Let the evidence determine whether a main chart needs most of a page or multiple comparable figures should share it. Keep axes/legends whenever needed for interpretation.

Use consistent title, caption and source positions; unequal evidence/explanation columns are often useful. Equal columns are appropriate for genuinely parallel options. Colors, typefaces and navigation follow the selected visual system, not a compulsory consulting template.

## Acceptance

Can the reader identify the decision, inspect its evidence, compare alternatives and see the recommendation's limits? Does each prominent number have a basis? Use the shared delivery checks; this scenario does not require a particular chart DSL or a fixed amount of content.
