# Academic authoring: from understanding to explanation

Use for new academic talks and substantive report rewrites. Respect a requested
outline, duration, page count, language and edit boundary. Do not redesign an
entire deck for a local correction. This workflow uses ordinary working notes;
it adds neither compiler fields nor a mandatory user approval step.

## 1. Select the report from the source

Identify the audience, time available, purpose of this report and the conclusion
or question the audience should carry away. Infer these from the request when
reasonable and state consequential assumptions. Ask only when the missing
information would materially change the report.

A paper-reading summary preserves what the author did. A talk selects what this
audience needs to understand and discuss. Keep requested comprehensive reading
notes as a separate deliverable; do not use their length or section count to
determine slide count. A methods seminar may need a long derivation; a progress
meeting may need one result and a carefully framed unresolved question.

Before page YAML, write a compact provisional outline in working notes. Refine
near-term pages while making them; readable figures and text can require splitting
or merging the initial plan. For each planned page, identify the audience's question, the answer or necessary setup, the
specific evidence/reasoning, and why the next page follows. Note the relationship
to show (for example correspondence, comparison or time evolution), rather than
choosing a two-column template first. Record what goes to spoken explanation,
notes or backup. These are thinking prompts, not boxes whose completion proves
quality and not text to print on slides.

Read the outline continuously. Repair jumps, redundant claims and unsupported
conclusions before layout. If two pages differ only in which source paragraph
they repeat, combine or remove them unless the distinction serves the audience.
Setup pages need a clear purpose, not a fabricated finding. Preserve explicit
user requests for exhaustive coverage, but organize that coverage intelligibly.

## 2. Resolve the difficult explanation before scaling a layout

Choose representative pages where understanding could fail: a physical model,
a central result, a derivation or an unresolved problem. Work out their content
and visual relationship before mass-producing the remaining pages. A small
report may need only one such page. This is not a fixed slide quota.

Use [worked examples](academic-page-examples.md) to distinguish a tidy but weak
page from an explanation. They are composition briefs, not mandatory templates
or verified scientific results.

For each representative page, ask what the audience should be able to point to
or explain after seeing it. Choose a supported representation of that relation:

- Physical object to mathematical model: map pictured components to symbols.
- Comparison: align the conditions and the quantities being compared; parallel
  panels are useful here, but do not imply that unlike concepts are peers.
- Result: give the key evidence enough space and identify the relevant trend,
  location or contrast with a short annotation and interpretation.
- Derivation: expose the step and its assumptions; move unused algebra to backup.
- Diagnosis: distinguish the observation from possible explanations and show
  what next observation would separate those explanations.

Reuse layouts when the relationship really repeats. Do not demand arbitrary
visual variety, and do not mistake repeated equal panels for logical structure.
Color, proximity, arrows and masks should encode a relationship or direct
attention; explanatory space takes priority over decorative frames.

Use actual supported native elements and supplied or appropriately prepared
assets. A missing diagram requires preparing the needed explanation or stating
the draft's limitation, not inventing data or substituting unrelated decoration.

## 3. Make evidence understandable on the page

For each important figure, identify the panel, axes/units, conditions, comparison
and feature that supports the sentence being stated. Preserve the necessary
labels and qualifications. Use a contextual view plus a labeled detail when
needed; enlarging or annotating a source figure must not change its data or
silently compare different scales. Do not force one whole paper figure per page
when its panels answer different questions.

Equations are explanatory objects. Use [equation routes](equations.md) to choose
actual Office Math or typeset high-resolution PNG assets, preserve source meaning and verify
the consumer render. Do not substitute ordinary text or handmade SVG text.

Define unfamiliar terms in the report language and keep established notation
consistent. Resolve ambiguous technical words by the defined observable or object: for
example, two-state populations and their difference are not demographic
population. Keep a short source-term/meaning/report-term mapping where ambiguity
exists, and check its use across final slides, notes and any reading report.
A correction on one visible page does not clear the other outputs.
Bilingual labels can help when needed; avoid replacing ordinary
explanation with mixed-language fragments merely copied from the source.

Keep a provenance/uncertainty record in working material. Put a qualification
on the slide when it changes the interpretation; reserve incidental audit
details for notes or backup. Production metadata, model names and statements
about how thoroughly the agent read the paper do not belong in an ordinary
scientific talk unless requested. Sources should identify the paper, figure or
result; local paths alone are usually not useful to the audience.

## 4. Review the report, not just the objects

Use [page and deck review](page-and-deck-review.md) for the distinct checks of
page logic, spatial explanation and whole-report continuity. Review difficult
representative pages before propagating a design; integrate accepted pages and
review the actual sequence afterward. Use the [single-page loop](page-edit.md)
for local failures, preserving satisfactory pages and following only affected
dependencies. [Delivery checks](delivery-checks.md) attach those observations to
the final file.

When work is delegated, the integrating agent must inspect the actual artifact;
a subagent's “QA passed” is not review evidence. This reference does not grant
permission to delegate. Keep independent first drafts separate from versions
repaired using reviewer instructions when evaluating Skill behavior.

Report what the reviewed artifacts establish. A Skill update, successful export
or corrected example does not establish reliable academic-authoring quality
across tasks. Behavior on representative work still needs to be observed.
